How often you need a penetration test depends on who is asking. Compliance frameworks set minimum frequencies, but threat exposure and change velocity often demand more.

## Compliance minimums by framework

| Framework | Minimum frequency |
|---|---|
| PCI DSS v4.0 | Annual and after significant changes |
| SOC 2 | Annual (auditor expectation) |
| HIPAA | Annual (implied by Security Rule evaluation) |
| ISO 27001 | Annual (auditor expectation) |
| NIST 800-171 | Annual (periodic assessment required) |
| CMMC 2.0 Level 2 | Annual |
| GLBA Safeguards Rule | At least annually |
| FedRAMP | Annual for Moderate/High impact levels |

## When annual testing is not enough

An annual test is the baseline. Consider more frequent testing if:

- **You deploy frequently.** SaaS companies releasing weekly or daily should test at least quarterly.
- **Your attack surface changes often.** New subdomains, APIs, cloud services, or acquisitions expand the surface.
- **You have a high-risk profile.** Fintech, healthcare, and defense companies benefit from more frequent testing.
- **You are preparing for an audit.** A pentest 2-3 months before a scheduled audit ensures you have time to remediate findings.
- **You had a major breach or security incident.** Post-incident testing validates that remediation was effective.

## The case for continuous testing

Continuous penetration testing programs provide ongoing validation rather than a single annual snapshot. Benefits include:

- **Faster detection** of regressions introduced by new releases
- **Better alignment** with agile development cycles
- **Reduced audit stress** — always have a current report available
- **Lower per-test cost** through subscription or credit-based pricing
