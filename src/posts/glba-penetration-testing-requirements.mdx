The FTC's Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) requires financial institutions to develop, implement, and maintain an information security program — and explicitly mandates penetration testing as part of that program.

## Who must comply

The Safeguards Rule applies to any "financial institution" as defined by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act. This includes:

- Banks, credit unions, and mortgage brokers
- Non-bank lenders and payday lenders
- Insurance companies and agents
- Securities and commodities firms
- Financial advisors and tax preparers
- Any business significantly engaged in financial activities

## What the Safeguards Rule requires for pentesting

The rule requires financial institutions to:

1. **Conduct periodic penetration testing** of their information systems.
2. **Perform vulnerability assessments** regularly.
3. **Document results** and use them to adjust the information security program.
4. **Involve qualified personnel** — internal or external — in testing.
5. **Address findings** in a timely manner.

## Scoping your GLBA pentest

Your pentest should cover all information systems that access, process, or store customer information. For most financial institutions, this includes:

- Customer-facing applications (online banking, loan applications, portals)
- Internal financial systems (account management, payment processing)
- APIs connecting to third-party financial services
- Employee workstations and internal networks
- Mobile banking applications

## Frequency requirements

The Safeguards Rule requires "periodic" testing. Industry best practice and auditor expectations align with at least annual testing for most financial institutions. Organizations with higher risk profiles or significant change velocity should test more frequently.
